## TL;DR for agents

Fix the **FM structure** (operator layout and frequency ratios), then learn only the **envelopes**, with the
**maximum modulation index bounded**. A few operators can be enough (2 operators matched trumpet). For the
YM2612: choose algorithm + MULT/DT first, then fit envelopes and levels with modulator TL bounded. Two-operator
fits also make CH3 special-mode "two voices on one channel" practical.

## Problem

Neural audio synthesis (such as DDSP) sounds good but its parameters aren't controllable like a synth's. FM is
compact and controllable but hard to drive from audio. Standard spectral reconstruction losses give **little
guidance on pitch/frequency ratios**, so optimizing FM jointly over ratios and envelopes struggles.

## Method

- **Differentiable FM synth:** a few sine oscillators (configurations tested: 6, 4 and 2 operators; for 4, the
  "Y", 4×1 and 2×2 layouts).
- **Fixed structure:** the FM configuration and frequency ratios are **fixed per instrument**; only the operator
  **envelopes** (output levels and modulation indices over time) are predicted.
- **Decoder:** a temporal convolutional network (about **400k parameters**, versus 4.5M for the DDSP baseline),
  conditioned on **pitch (f0) and loudness** frame sequences.
- **I<sub>max</sub>:** the maximum modulation index is a hyperparameter. A poor value prevents convergence or
  gives unnatural results.
- A **learnable reverb** models the room.
- **Loss:** standard multi-scale spectral reconstruction.

## Results

- Data: URMP dataset; instruments violin, flute and trumpet.
- Quality comparable to the DDSP "harmonic plus noise" baseline by **Fréchet Audio Distance**.
- **Trumpet: a 2-operator model beat the baseline.** Violin and flute benefited from more operators.
- Real time on a laptop CPU (i7-6700HQ) at **32 ms** latency (real-time factor 0.637); the baseline needs 128 ms.
- **Interpretable:** changing learned ratios or envelope amplitudes after training changes the sound
  predictably, without retraining.

## Limitations

- Three instruments, evaluated on excerpts.
- Sensitive to I<sub>max</sub>; reverb estimation can fail; analysis of both left to future work.
- Intervention (editing parameters after training) is a proof of concept.

## What applies to Mod2Xgm

| Paper idea | Mod2Xgm decision |
|---|---|
| Fix structure, learn envelopes | Two-step fit: choose **algorithm + MULT/DT** first, then fit **TL, FB, AR/DR/SL/D2R/RR** (T8.2) |
| Bound I<sub>max</sub> | Bound **modulator TL** (minimum attenuation) and **FB** during fitting, rather than let the search explore extreme modulation |
| Pitch and loudness conditioning | Fit per **pitch region**; MOD volume and effects map to TL changes at playback, not to new patches |
| 2 operators can be enough | Try 2-operator fits first for simple timbres. They enable **CH3 special mode**: two 2-operator voices on one channel, for locked instrument pairs ([../multi-pass.md](../multi-pass.md#candidates)) |
| Differentiable synth + neural decoder | **Not used.** The YM2612 plays a *static patch* per note (plus TL/frequency changes); there's no per-frame neural control at playback. Derivative-free search over integer parameters is the right tool |
| Learnable reverb | Not applicable. MOD samples may contain baked-in reverb, which FM fits poorly; another reason such samples stay PCM |
| Interpretability | Fitted patches should be readable and editable in DefleMask (DMF output); prefer simple, sane patches when scores tie |
