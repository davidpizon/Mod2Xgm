# ADR 0057: SN76489 model: a bit-exact port of MAME's Sega PSG, checked against Nuked-PSG (T7.3)

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-10-30

## Context

Plan task T7.3: the optimizer has to hear what its PSG candidates (square tones and noise) sound like on a Genesis,
inside the converter, with no native library for it ([ADR 0010](0010-multi-pass-optimizer-sgdk-compliance-pure-csharp.md)).
The plan asked for "tests vs reference captures".

The Genesis's PSG is not a TI chip: it is Sega's copy inside the VDP (the YM7101 on later boards). It differs from
TI's SN76489 in its noise (a 16-bit shift register tapped at bits 0 and 3), in what a tone period of 0 does, and in
its power-on state. The sources also disagree on a few points: SMS Power says periods 0 and 1 hold the output still,
MAME and Genesis Plus GX flip it on every step.

Candidates to copy: MAME's `sn76496.cpp` (BSD-3-Clause), Genesis Plus GX's `psg.c` (no commercial use: not allowed),
Nuked-PSG (GPL-2.0+, a model of the YM7101's PSG built from its die shot), or writing one from the SMS Power notes.

## Decision

- **Port MAME's Sega PSG** (David). `src/Render/Psg/Sn76489.cs` is a C# port of MAME's `sn76496.cpp` at commit
  `d066f16`, cut down to the `segapsg` setup: feedback into bit 15, white-noise taps at bits 0 and 3, the output
  negated, a ÷16 clock, a period of 0 kept as 0, the latch starting on register 3 and every volume at 15 at power-on.
  The TI and NCR variants, the Game Gear's stereo register and the READY line are left out.
- **The port is bit-exact with MAME, mistakes included** (David). Where MAME differs from the chip, the port does
  too, and the difference is recorded below. Fixing those is a new plan task, T7.8, one at a time, each measured,
  like the YM3438's T7.7.
- **The output is the chip's own rate** (David): one mono sample of integers every 16 PSG clocks, the rate its
  counters step at (223 722 Hz on NTSC, 221 681 Hz on PAL). Each sample is the sum of the four channels' levels where
  their output is high, negated as MAME has it: 0 down to −32 764 (4 × 8191). Volume 0 is 8191, each step 2 dB less,
  15 is off. Writes take effect before the next sample. Resampling, the DC offset this unipolar output has, and the
  level against the FM chip are for the Genesis renderer (T7.5).
- **Nuked-PSG is the independent check** (David approved the GPL for a test tool only). Nuked-PSG (GPL-2.0+, commit
  `d15a168`) models the YM7101's PSG gate by gate, so it stands in for the real chip. It is used only by a test tool,
  never shipped, and none of its code is copied into the converter.
- **Reference outputs are made once and checked in**, as for the YM3438. `tools/sn76489-reference/run.sh` downloads
  MAME's two PSG files and Nuked-PSG at the pinned commits, builds `reference.cpp` with g++ (MAME's file compiles
  unchanged against a small stand-in for MAME's device framework, `shim/emu.h`), plays every probe script in
  `tests/golden/sn76489/` through both, and writes `<probe>.mame.gz` and `<probe>.nuked.gz` (gzipped little-endian
  int32s, 16 KB for all nine probes). CI needs no C++ compiler.
- **Probe scripts are text** (`w value`, `s samples`), built in code by `Sn76489Probes` and kept current by a test.
  Nine probes cover a plain tone, all sixteen volumes, every kind of period (the top, the bottom, 0 and 1, one byte at
  a time during a note), a chord with all four channels, white and periodic noise at every rate and following tone 3,
  the noise restart, the latch's write quirks and the power-on latch.
- **Driving Nuked:** its stream is the chip's pins, so the tool clocks it at the PSG clock and reads it after every
  16th clock, ×8192 as Nuked's own `YMPSG_Generate` does. A write takes 2 clocks to land (the chip latches the byte,
  then applies it), so each run of writes is scheduled to end on the sample boundary where the script puts it, and the
  pre-roll starts 1024 clocks before the first write, because the chip ignores writes for about 30 clocks after reset.
  Nuked's output is positive; the sign doesn't matter to the comparison.

## The checks

| Test | Bar |
|---|---|
| Port vs MAME, every probe | Identical, sample for sample (it is) |
| Port vs Nuked-PSG, log-mel distance after averaging every 4 samples (55.9 kHz) | 0.3 dB, or a per-probe bar for a known difference (below) |

Averaging four samples keeps most mel bands in the audible range; at 224 kHz a third of them would be above 20 kHz.

Measured against Nuked-PSG when the bars were set:

| Probe | dB | Bar |
|---|---|---|
| tone | 0.03 | 0.3 |
| chord | 0.84 | 1.0, known differences 1 and 3 |
| periods | 0.89 | 1.1, known difference 2 |
| write-quirks | 0.98 | 1.2, known differences 2 and 3 |
| noise-restart | 1.90 | 2.3, known difference 1 |
| volumes | 2.07 | 2.5, known difference 1 |
| white-noise | 2.07 | 2.5, known difference 3 |
| periodic-noise | 2.98 | 3.5, known difference 3 |
| power-on-latch | 9.98 | 11.0, known difference 4 |

What the two agree on: the tone and noise rates, a period of 0 or 1 flipping on every step (neither holds the output
still, as SMS Power says), the noise's bit sequence (the same bits, white and periodic), the noise restart on every
write to its register, data bytes going to the latched register, and the loudest level (8191 vs 8192).

## Known differences from the chip

Found by comparing MAME with Nuked-PSG. Each is in MAME, so it is in the port; T7.8 closes them.

1. **Volume levels.** MAME's levels are exact 2 dB steps. Nuked-PSG's own table (1.0, 0.772, 0.622, 0.485, 0.382,
   0.29, 0.229, 0.174, 0.132, 0.096, 0.072, 0.051, 0.034, 0.019, 0.009, 0) falls off faster at the quiet end: about
   0.25 dB below MAME at volume 1, 3 dB at volume 10 and 13 dB at volume 14. **Verify** where Nuked's table comes
   from (its source gives no reference); quiet PSG notes and PSG fades depend on it.
2. **When a period change takes effect.** The chip counts up and flips when the count reaches the period, so a new,
   shorter period flips at once if the count is already past it, and a longer one stretches the current half-wave.
   MAME counts the old half-wave down first. A change can land up to one half-wave (4.6 ms at the lowest note) apart,
   which matters for PSG vibrato and slides, which change the period every frame.
3. **The noise's timing.** The chip clears its shift register at once on a write to the noise register and shifts on
   the rising edge of its rate counter (or of tone 3); MAME keeps the last bit until its next shift. The bits are the
   same, but they start up to one shift apart (more when following a slow tone 3), so short noise bursts (drums)
   differ.
4. **The register the latch points at after power-on.** MAME starts on register 3 (tone 2's volume), from hardware
   tests credited in its source; Nuked starts on register 0 only because its whole state is cleared, not from its
   reset logic. Songs always latch a register before writing data, so only a probe sees it. **Verify** before
   following Nuked here.

## Options considered

- **Port MAME's Sega PSG, check against Nuked-PSG (chosen).**
- **Write one from the SMS Power notes.** No copied code, but nothing to be bit-exact with, and the notes disagree
  with both emulators on periods 0 and 1.
- **Port Nuked-PSG.** Closest to the chip, but GPL: it can't be shipped in the MIT converter.
- **Port Genesis Plus GX's `psg.c`.** Its license forbids commercial use.
- **Fix the known differences in this task.** More chip-like sooner, but no longer checkable against MAME there;
  moved to T7.8 so each fix is measured on its own (David).
- **Only MAME as the check.** No GPL tool, but no evidence of how the real chip sounds.
- **Output at the FM chip's rate (53 kHz).** Easier to mix, but the model would have to filter and average itself,
  which is less exact and harder to check; the Genesis renderer (T7.5) resamples instead.

## Consequences

- `THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md` reproduces MAME's BSD-3 notice for `sn76496.cpp` (shipped) and lists Nuked-PSG and MAME's
  C++ (test tool only). AGENTS.md lists the approvals.
- Changing a probe means: `UPDATE_GOLDEN=1 dotnet test` to rewrite the scripts, then `tools/sn76489-reference/run.sh`.
- The model has no idea of time beyond samples: the Genesis renderer (T7.5) owns the clock, the write timing,
  resampling, the DC offset and the PSG's level against the FM chip.
- New plan task T7.8 closes the four differences. Each fix leaves the port bit-exact with MAME everywhere else and
  lowers that probe's Nuked bar.
